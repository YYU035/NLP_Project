Corpus document 24241932: Ecological analysis of ethnic differences in relation between tuberculosis and poverty.

Paper verdict: CONTRADICTED → INSUFFICIENT.

Original abstract; yellow marks benchmark rationale sentences
  1. OBJECTIVE To examine the effect of ethnicity on the relation between tuberculosis and deprivation.
  2. DESIGN Retrospective ecological study comparing incidence of tuberculosis in white and south Asian residents of the 39 electoral wards in Birmingham with ethnic specific indices of deprivation.
  3. SETTING Birmingham, 1989-93.
  4. SUBJECTS 1516 notified cases of tuberculosis.
  5. MAIN OUTCOME MEASURES Rates of tuberculosis and measures of deprivation.
  6. RESULTS Univariate analysis showed significant associations of tuberculosis rates for the whole population with several indices of deprivation (P<0.01) and with the proportion of the population of south Asian origin (P<0.01).
  7. All deprivation covariates were positively associated with each other but on multiple regression, higher level of overcrowding was independently associated with tuberculosis rates.
  8. For the white population, overcrowding was associated with tuberculosis rates independently of other variables (P=0.0036).
  9. No relation with deprivation was found for the south Asian population in either single or multivariable analyses.
  10. CONCLUSIONS Poverty is significantly associated with tuberculosis in the white population, but no such relation exists for those of Asian ethnicity.
  11. These findings suggest that causal factors, and therefore potential interventions, will also differ by ethnic group.

Corpus document 10365749: Neighbourhood risk factors for tuberculosis in Hong Kong.

Paper verdict: INSUFFICIENT → INSUFFICIENT.

Original abstract; yellow marks benchmark rationale sentences
  1. BACKGROUND Tuberculosis (TB) has been reported to be associated with poverty, especially in developing countries.
  2. Hong Kong is one of the few industrialised areas with a high incidence of TB where previous reports on the effect of poverty at neighbourhood level have been conflicting.
  3. OBJECTIVE To examine the spatial distribution of TB and its association with neighbourhood risk factors.
  4. METHOD A total of 17 294 TB cases notified from 2005 to 2007 were mapped down to the District Council Constituency Area (DCCA) level, and were indirectly standardised by age and sex using 2006 census population data.
  5. The standardised TB ratio was correlated with neighbourhood risk factors classified by family, ethnicity, economic and environmental domains.
  6. RESULTS The indirect age- and sex-standardised ratio demonstrated a spatially varied pattern, and was significantly associated with all neighbourhood factors on univariate analysis.
  7. Only marital status, place of birth and low household income were independently associated with the standardised TB ratio on multivariate analysis.
  8. CONCLUSION Despite the virtual elimination of absolute poverty by a well-developed social assistance scheme, low household income in the neighbourhood was significantly associated with TB, independently of place of birth, marital status and other risk factors.

Corpus document 22414304: Treatment outcome of an unselected cohort of tuberculosis patients in relation to human immunodeficiency virus serostatus in Zomba Hospital, Malawi.

Paper verdict: INSUFFICIENT → INSUFFICIENT. Gate removed all candidate passages.

Original abstract; yellow marks benchmark rationale sentences
  1. There is little information about treatment outcome in patients with smear-negative pulmonary tuberculosis (PTB) or extrapulmonary tuberculosis (EPTB) treated under routine programme conditions in subsaharan Africa.
  2. A prospective study was carried out to determine treatment outcome in an unselected cohort of TB patients admitted to Zomba General Hospital, Malawi.
  3. Eight hundred and twenty-seven adult TB patients (451 men and 376 women) were registered between 1 July and 31 December 1995.
  4. Standardized treatment outcomes of treatment completion, death, default, and transfer to another district were assessed in relation to type of TB, human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) serostatus, age and gender.
  5. Two hundred and fifty-four patients (31%) died by the end of treatment, half of the deaths occurring in the first month.
  6. Death rates were 19% among 386 patients with smear-positive PTB, 46% among 211 patients with smear-negative PTB, and 37% among 230 patients with EPTB; 77% of the patients were HIV seropositive.
  7. Among new patients, HIV-positive patients had higher death rates than HIV-negative patients (hazard ratio [HR] 2.5; 95% confidence interval [95% CI] 1.6-3.8).
  8. Smear-negative patients had the highest death rates (HR 3.9; 95% CI 2.7-5.5 compared to smear-positive patients), followed by EPTB patients (HR 2.6, 95% CI 1.8-3.7 compared to smear-positive patients).
  9. Death rates increased with age but were similar in men and women.
  10. Adult patients in Malawi with smear-negative PTB and EPTB have low treatment completion and high death rates, related to high levels of HIV infection.
  11. National TB control programmes in areas of high HIV prevalence should no longer ignore treatment outcomes in patients with smear-negative PTB or EPTB.

Corpus document 5284188: Alarming levels of drug-resistant tuberculosis in Belarus: results of a survey in Minsk

Paper verdict: INSUFFICIENT → INSUFFICIENT. Gate removed all candidate passages.

Original abstract; yellow marks benchmark rationale sentences
  1. Resistance to anti-tuberculosis (TB) medicines is a major public health threat in most countries of the former Soviet Union.
  2. As no representative and quality-assured information on the magnitude of this problem existed in Belarus, a survey was conducted in the capital city of Minsk.
  3. Between November 2009 and December 2010, 156 consecutively diagnosed new and 68 previously treated culture-positive TB patients residing in Minsk were enrolled in the survey.
  4. Mycobacterium tuberculosis isolates were obtained from each patient and tested for susceptibility to first- and second-line anti-TB drugs.
  5. Multidrug-resistant (MDR)-TB was found in 35.3% (95% CI 27.7-42.8) of new patients and 76.5% (95% CI 66.1-86.8) of those previously treated.
  6. Overall, nearly one in two patients enrolled had MDR-TB.
  7. Extensively drug-resistant TB was reported in 15 of the 107 MDR-TB patients (14.0%, 95% CI 7.3-20.7).
  8. Patients <35 yrs of age have shown a two times higher odds ratio of multidrug-resistant TB than those aged >35 yrs.
  9. The findings of this survey in Minsk city are alarming and represent the highest proportions of MDR-TB ever recorded in the world.
  10. This study greatly contributes to the understanding of the burden of drug-resistant TB in urban areas of Belarus.

Corpus document 7111021: Integration of antiretroviral therapy with tuberculosis treatment.

Paper verdict: INSUFFICIENT → INSUFFICIENT. Gate removed all candidate passages.

Original abstract; yellow marks benchmark rationale sentences
  1. BACKGROUND We previously reported that integrating antiretroviral therapy (ART) with tuberculosis treatment reduces mortality.
  2. However, the timing for the initiation of ART during tuberculosis treatment remains unresolved.
  3. METHODS We conducted a three-group, open-label, randomized, controlled trial in South Africa involving 642 ambulatory patients, all with tuberculosis (confirmed by a positive sputum smear for acid-fast bacilli), human immunodeficiency virus infection, and a CD4+ T-cell count of less than 500 per cubic millimeter.
  4. Findings in the earlier-ART group (ART initiated within 4 weeks after the start of tuberculosis treatment, 214 patients) and later-ART group (ART initiated during the first 4 weeks of the continuation phase of tuberculosis treatment, 215 patients) are presented here.
  5. RESULTS At baseline, the median CD4+ T-cell count was 150 per cubic millimeter, and the median viral load was 161,000 copies per milliliter, with no significant differences between the two groups.
  6. The incidence rate of the acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) or death was 6.9 cases per 100 person-years in the earlier-ART group (18 cases) as compared with 7.8 per 100 person-years in the later-ART group (19 cases) (incidence-rate ratio, 0.89; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.44 to 1.79; P=0.73).
  7. However, among patients with CD4+ T-cell counts of less than 50 per cubic millimeter, the incidence rates of AIDS or death were 8.5 and 26.3 cases per 100 person-years, respectively (incidence-rate ratio, 0.32; 95% CI, 0.07 to 1.13; P=0.06).
  8. The incidence rates of the immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS) were 20.1 and 7.7 cases per 100 person-years, respectively (incidence-rate ratio, 2.62; 95% CI, 1.48 to 4.82; P<0.001).
  9. Adverse events requiring a switching of antiretroviral drugs occurred in 10 patients in the earlier-ART group and 1 patient in the later-ART group (P=0.006).
  10. CONCLUSIONS Early initiation of ART in patients with CD4+ T-cell counts of less than 50 per cubic millimeter increased AIDS-free survival.
  11. Deferral of the initiation of ART to the first 4 weeks of the continuation phase of tuberculosis therapy in those with higher CD4+ T-cell counts reduced the risks of IRIS and other adverse events related to ART without increasing the risk of AIDS or death.
  12. (Funded by the U.S. President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief and others; SAPIT ClinicalTrials.gov number, NCT00398996.).
