Corpus document 22544171: Reversal of the cellular phenotype in the premature aging disease Hutchinson-Gilford progeria syndrome

Paper verdict: INSUFFICIENT → INSUFFICIENT.

Original abstract; yellow marks benchmark rationale sentences
  1. Hutchinson-Gilford progeria syndrome (HGPS) is a childhood premature aging disease caused by a spontaneous point mutation in lamin A (encoded by LMNA), one of the major architectural elements of the mammalian cell nucleus.
  2. The HGPS mutation activates an aberrant cryptic splice site in LMNA pre-mRNA, leading to synthesis of a truncated lamin A protein and concomitant reduction in wild-type lamin A. Fibroblasts from individuals with HGPS have severe morphological abnormalities in nuclear envelope structure.
  3. Here we show that the cellular disease phenotype is reversible in cells from individuals with HGPS.
  4. Introduction of wild-type lamin A protein does not rescue the cellular disease symptoms.
  5. The mutant LMNA mRNA and lamin A protein can be efficiently eliminated by correction of the aberrant splicing event using a modified oligonucleotide targeted to the activated cryptic splice site.
  6. Upon splicing correction, HGPS fibroblasts assume normal nuclear morphology, the aberrant nuclear distribution and cellular levels of lamina-associated proteins are rescued, defects in heterochromatin-specific histone modifications are corrected and proper expression of several misregulated genes is reestablished.
  7. Our results establish proof of principle for the correction of the premature aging phenotype in individuals with HGPS.

Corpus document 15692098: Hutchinson-Gilford progeria syndrome: review of the phenotype

Paper verdict: INSUFFICIENT → INSUFFICIENT.

Original abstract; yellow marks benchmark rationale sentences
  1. Hutchinson-Gilford progeria syndrome (HGPS) is a rare but well known entity characterized by extreme short stature, low body weight, early loss of hair, lipodystrophy, scleroderma, decreased joint mobility, osteolysis, and facial features that resemble aged persons.
  2. Cardiovascular compromise leads to early demise.
  3. Cognitive development is normal.
  4. Data on 10 of our own cases and 132 cases from literature are presented.
  5. The incidence in the last century in the Netherlands was 1:4,000,000.
  6. Sex ratio was 1.2:1.
  7. Main first symptoms were failure to thrive (55%), hair loss (40%), skin problems (28%), and lipodystrophy (20%).
  8. Mean age at diagnosis was 2.9 years.
  9. Growth in weight was more disturbed than growth in height, and growth delay started already prenatally.
  10. Mean height > 13 years was 109.0 cm, mean weight was 14.5 kg.
  11. Osteolysis was wide-spread but not expressed, except in the viscerocranium, and remained limited to membranous formed bone.
  12. Lipodystrophy is generalized, only intra-abdominal fat depositions remain present.
  13. Cardiovascular problems are extremely variable, both in age of onset and nature.
  14. Stroke and coronary dysfunctioning are most frequent.
  15. Pathologic findings in coronaries and aorta resemble sometimes the findings in elderly persons, but can also be much more limited.
  16. Loss of smooth muscle cells seems the most important finding.
  17. Mean age of demise was 12.6 years.
  18. Patients can be subdivided in patients with classical HGPS, which follows an autosomal dominant pattern of inheritance, (almost) all cases representing spontaneous mutations, and in non-classical progeria, in whom growth can be less retarded, scalp hair remains present for a longer time, lipodystrophy is more slowly progressive, osteolysis is more expressed except in the face, and survival well into adulthood is not uncommon.
  19. Pattern of inheritance of non-classical progeria is most probably autosomal recessive.
  20. The cause of HGPS is an abnormally formed Lamin A, either directly by a mutated LMNA gene, or through abnormal posttranslational processing (ZMPSTE24 gene mutations).
  21. Of 34 LMNA mutations found in progeria patients, there were 26 classical p.
  22. G608G mutations (76%).
  23. Pathogenesis is most likely to follow several different pathways.
  24. Potential therapeutic strategies are developed along these lines and include RNA interference techniques and inhibition of the dominant-negative influence of abnormally formed Lamin A on polymerization with normally formed Lamin A.

Corpus document 14178995: Summary

Paper verdict: INSUFFICIENT → INSUFFICIENT.

Original abstract; yellow marks benchmark rationale sentences
  1. The genetic diseases Hutchinson-Gilford progeria syndrome (HGPS) and restrictive dermopathy (RD) arise from accumulation of farnesylated prelamin A because of defects in the lamin A maturation pathway.
  2. Both of these diseases exhibit symptoms that can be viewed as accelerated aging.
  3. The mechanism by which accumulation of farnesylated prelamin A leads to these accelerated aging phenotypes is not understood.
  4. Here we present evidence that in HGPS and RD fibroblasts, DNA damage checkpoints are persistently activated because of the compromise in genomic integrity.
  5. Inactivation of checkpoint kinases Ataxia-telangiectasia-mutated (ATM) and ATR (ATM- and Rad3-related) in these patient cells can partially overcome their early replication arrest.
  6. Treatment of patient cells with a protein farnesyltransferase inhibitor (FTI) did not result in reduction of DNA double-strand breaks and damage checkpoint signaling, although the treatment significantly reversed the aberrant shape of their nuclei.
  7. This suggests that DNA damage accumulation and aberrant nuclear morphology are independent phenotypes arising from prelamin A accumulation in these progeroid syndromes.
  8. Since DNA damage accumulation is an important contributor to the symptoms of HGPS, our results call into question the possibility of treatment of HGPS with FTIs alone.

Corpus document 34747208: Lamin A-dependent nuclear defects in human aging.

Paper verdict: INSUFFICIENT → INSUFFICIENT. Gate removed all candidate passages.

Original abstract; yellow marks benchmark rationale sentences
  1. Mutations in the nuclear structural protein lamin A cause the premature aging syndrome Hutchinson-Gilford progeria (HGPS).
  2. Whether lamin A plays any role in normal aging is unknown.
  3. We show that the same molecular mechanism responsible for HGPS is active in healthy cells.
  4. Cell nuclei from old individuals acquire defects similar to those of HGPS patient cells, including changes in histone modifications and increased DNA damage.
  5. Age-related nuclear defects are caused by sporadic use, in healthy individuals, of the same cryptic splice site in lamin A whose constitutive activation causes HGPS.
  6. Inhibition of this splice site reverses the nuclear defects associated with aging.
  7. These observations implicate lamin A in physiological aging.
