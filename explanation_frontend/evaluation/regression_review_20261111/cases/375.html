Corpus document 1522647: Circulating Mitochondrial DNA in Patients in the ICU as a Marker of Mortality: Derivation and Validation

Paper verdict: SUPPORTED → INSUFFICIENT. Gate removed all candidate passages.

Original abstract; yellow marks benchmark rationale sentences
  1. BACKGROUND Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) is a critical activator of inflammation and the innate immune system.
  2. However, mtDNA level has not been tested for its role as a biomarker in the intensive care unit (ICU).
  3. We hypothesized that circulating cell-free mtDNA levels would be associated with mortality and improve risk prediction in ICU patients.
  4. METHODS AND FINDINGS Analyses of mtDNA levels were performed on blood samples obtained from two prospective observational cohort studies of ICU patients (the Brigham and Women's Hospital Registry of Critical Illness [BWH RoCI, n = 200] and Molecular Epidemiology of Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome [ME ARDS, n = 243]).
  5. mtDNA levels in plasma were assessed by measuring the copy number of the NADH dehydrogenase 1 gene using quantitative real-time PCR.
  6. Medical ICU patients with an elevated mtDNA level (≥3,200 copies/µl plasma) had increased odds of dying within 28 d of ICU admission in both the BWH RoCI (odds ratio [OR] 7.5, 95% CI 3.6-15.8, p = 1×10(-7)) and ME ARDS (OR 8.4, 95% CI 2.9-24.2, p = 9×10(-5)) cohorts, while no evidence for association was noted in non-medical ICU patients.
  7. The addition of an elevated mtDNA level improved the net reclassification index (NRI) of 28-d mortality among medical ICU patients when added to clinical models in both the BWH RoCI (NRI 79%, standard error 14%, p<1×10(-4)) and ME ARDS (NRI 55%, standard error 20%, p = 0.007) cohorts.
  8. In the BWH RoCI cohort, those with an elevated mtDNA level had an increased risk of death, even in analyses limited to patients with sepsis or acute respiratory distress syndrome.
  9. Study limitations include the lack of data elucidating the concise pathological roles of mtDNA in the patients, and the limited numbers of measurements for some of biomarkers.
  10. CONCLUSIONS Increased mtDNA levels are associated with ICU mortality, and inclusion of mtDNA level improves risk prediction in medical ICU patients.
  11. Our data suggest that mtDNA could serve as a viable plasma biomarker in medical ICU patients.

Corpus document 20459964: Circulating mitochondrial DNA in serum of patients with granulomatosis with polyangiitis.

Paper verdict: INSUFFICIENT → INSUFFICIENT.

Original abstract; yellow marks benchmark rationale sentences
  1. Neutrophil is a key cell in pathophysiology of granulomatosis with polyangiitis.
  2. Recently, neutrophil extracellular traps were described in this disease.
  3. Mitochondrial DNA is also released during traps formation.
  4. We measured circulating cell-free mitochondrial and genomic DNA in serum of patients with granulomatosis with polyangiitis.
  5. Subjects with the disease (14 active and 11 in remission stage) and 10 healthy controls were enrolled.
  6. Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR) was used to measure 79 base pairs (bp) and 230 bp mtDNA fragments.
  7. Alu repeats were quantified to evaluate abundance of nuclear DNA in serum at the presence of plasmid control.
  8. Both fragments of mtDNA (79 bp and 230 bp) and genomic DNA were elevated significantly in granulomatosis with polyangiitis compared to controls.
  9. Only the shorter 79 bp mtDNA correlated with active stage of granulomatosis with polyangiitis and clinical symptoms.
  10. A mechanism of extracellular release of mitochondrial DNA accompanies the active stage of the disease.
  11. Circulating mtDNA is extremely high in untreated patients.
  12. This suggests that biomarker properties of mtDNA are useful for monitoring of treatment.

Corpus document 3883485: Genetic Drift Can Compromise Mitochondrial Replacement by Nuclear Transfer in Human Oocytes.

Paper verdict: INSUFFICIENT → INSUFFICIENT. Gate removed all candidate passages.

Original abstract; yellow marks benchmark rationale sentences
  1. Replacement of mitochondria through nuclear transfer between oocytes of two different women has emerged recently as a strategy for preventing inheritance of mtDNA diseases.
  2. Although experiments in human oocytes have shown effective replacement, the consequences of small amounts of mtDNA carryover have not been studied sufficiently.
  3. Using human mitochondrial replacement stem cell lines, we show that, even though the low levels of heteroplasmy introduced into human oocytes by mitochondrial carryover during nuclear transfer often vanish, they can sometimes instead result in mtDNA genotypic drift and reversion to the original genotype.
  4. Comparison of cells with identical oocyte-derived nuclear DNA but different mtDNA shows that either mtDNA genotype is compatible with the nucleus and that drift is independent of mitochondrial function.
  5. Thus, although functional replacement of the mitochondrial genome is possible, even low levels of heteroplasmy can affect the stability of the mtDNA genotype and compromise the efficacy of mitochondrial replacement.

Corpus document 44693226: Effect of lipid restriction on mitochondrial free radical production and oxidative DNA damage.

Paper verdict: INSUFFICIENT → INSUFFICIENT. Gate removed all candidate passages.

Original abstract; yellow marks benchmark rationale sentences
  1. Many studies have shown that caloric restriction (40%) decreases mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation in rodents.
  2. Moreover, we have recently found that 7 weeks of 40% protein restriction without strong caloric restriction also decreases ROS production in rat liver.
  3. This is interesting since it has been reported that protein restriction can also extend longevity in rodents.
  4. In the present study we have investigated the possible role of dietary lipids in the effects of caloric restriction on mitochondrial oxidative stress.
  5. Using semipurified diets, the ingestion of lipids in male Wistar rats was decreased by 40% below controls, while the other dietary components were ingested at exactly the same level as in animals fed ad libitum.
  6. After 7 weeks of treatment the liver mitochondria of lipid-restricted animals showed significant increases in oxygen consumption with complex I-linked substrates (pyruvate/malate and glutamate/malate).
  7. Neither mitochondrial H(2)O(2) production nor oxidative damage to mitochondrial or nuclear DNA was modified in lipid-restricted animals.
  8. Oxidative damage to mitochondrial DNA was one order of magnitude higher than that of nuclear DNA in both dietary groups.
  9. These results deny a role for lipids and reinforce the possible role of dietary proteins as being responsible for the decrease in mitochondrial ROS production and DNA damage in caloric restriction.

Corpus document 28249680: BAX/BAK-independent mitoptosis during cell death induced by proteasome inhibition?

Paper verdict: INSUFFICIENT → INSUFFICIENT. Gate removed all candidate passages.

Original abstract; yellow marks benchmark rationale sentences
  1. Proteasome inhibitors induce rapid death of cancer cells.
  2. We show that in epithelial cancer cells, such death is associated with dramatic and simultaneous up-regulation of several BH3-only proteins, including BIK, BIM, MCL-1S, NOXA, and PUMA, as well as p53.
  3. Elevated levels of these proteins seem to be the result of direct inhibition of their proteasomal degradation, induction of transcription, and active translation.

We identify the cascade of molecular events responsible for cell death induced by a prototypical proteasome inhibitor, MG132, starting with rapid accumulation of BH3-only proteins in the mitochondria, proceeding through mitochondrial membrane permeabilization and subsequent loss of DeltaPsi(m), and leading to irreversible changes of mitochondrial ultrastructure, degradation of mitochondrial network, and detrimental impairment of crucial mitochondrial functions.
  6. Our results also establish a rationale for the broader use of proteasome inhibitors to kill apoptosis-resistant tumor cells that lack functional BAX/BAK proteins.
