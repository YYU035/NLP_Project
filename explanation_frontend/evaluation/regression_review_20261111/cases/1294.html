Corpus document 11011905: Pioneer Transcription Factors Target Partial DNA Motifs on Nucleosomes to Initiate Reprogramming

Paper verdict: CONTRADICTED → INSUFFICIENT. Gate removed all candidate passages.

Original abstract; yellow marks benchmark rationale sentences
  1. Pioneer transcription factors (TFs) access silent chromatin and initiate cell-fate changes, using diverse types of DNA binding domains (DBDs).
  2. FoxA, the paradigm pioneer TF, has a winged helix DBD that resembles linker histone and thereby binds its target sites on nucleosomes and in compacted chromatin.
  3. Herein, we compare the nucleosome and chromatin targeting activities of Oct4 (POU DBD), Sox2 (HMG box DBD), Klf4 (zinc finger DBD), and c-Myc (bHLH DBD), which together reprogram somatic cells to pluripotency.
  4. Purified Oct4, Sox2, and Klf4 proteins can bind nucleosomes in vitro, and in vivo they preferentially target silent sites enriched for nucleosomes.
  5. Pioneer activity relates simply to the ability of a given DBD to target partial motifs displayed on the nucleosome surface.
  6. Such partial motif recognition can occur by coordinate binding between factors.
  7. Our findings provide insight into how pioneer factors can target naive chromatin sites.

Corpus document 2151983: Epigenetic switch involved in activation of pioneer factor FOXA1-dependent enhancers.

Paper verdict: INSUFFICIENT → INSUFFICIENT.

Original abstract; yellow marks benchmark rationale sentences
  1. Transcription factors (TFs) bind specifically to discrete regions of mammalian genomes called cis-regulatory elements.
  2. Among those are enhancers, which play key roles in regulation of gene expression during development and differentiation.
  3. Despite the recognized central regulatory role exerted by chromatin in control of TF functions, much remains to be learned regarding the chromatin structure of enhancers and how it is established.
  4. Here, we have analyzed on a genomic-scale enhancers that recruit FOXA1, a pioneer transcription factor that triggers transcriptional competency of these cis-regulatory sites.
  5. Importantly, we found that FOXA1 binds to genomic regions showing local DNA hypomethylation and that its cell-type-specific recruitment to chromatin is linked to differential DNA methylation levels of its binding sites.
  6. Using neural differentiation as a model, we showed that induction of FOXA1 expression and its subsequent recruitment to enhancers is associated with DNA demethylation.
  7. Concomitantly, histone H3 lysine 4 methylation is induced at these enhancers.
  8. These epigenetic changes may both stabilize FOXA1 binding and allow for subsequent recruitment of transcriptional regulatory effectors.
  9. Interestingly, when cloned into reporter constructs, FOXA1-dependent enhancers were able to recapitulate their cell type specificity.
  10. However, their activities were inhibited by DNA methylation.
  11. Hence, these enhancers are intrinsic cell-type-specific regulatory regions of which activities have to be potentiated by FOXA1 through induction of an epigenetic switch that includes notably DNA demethylation.
